# Texas statutory posted notices — H2SEP

_Everything the State of Texas (and the federal government) makes a 115-key hotel post that
is **not** fire code, **not** ADA, and **not** pool rules — those live in sibling files.
Researched 2026-08-20 against statute and rule text._

**Jurisdictional facts established:**
- H2SEP is a **"commercial lodging establishment"** under Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §114.0001(1)
  (a hotel offering **more than 10 rooms**) → **Chapter 114 applies**.
- Food service is permitted and inspected by the **City of Eagle Pass Health Unit**
  (Community Development Dept., 3295 Bob Rogers Dr., **(830) 773-7781**) — **not DSHS**. The
  City adopts the Texas Food Establishment Rules (25 TAC Ch. 228).
- Texas has **no** statewide indoor smoking ban. **Eagle Pass does** have a comprehensive
  smoke-free ordinance (≈2010) — section number and signage spec **unverified**.

## Five more premises that were wrong

| Assumed | Actual |
|---|---|
| Human trafficking signage runs through Gov't Code §402.0351 | **Hotels are not on that list.** The duty is **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Ch. 114** (HB 390, 87th Leg., eff. 1/1/2022). §402.035 is just the Task Force — no signage duty. |
| Innkeeper valuables notice is in Civil Practice & Remedies Ch. 137 | **No such provision exists.** It is **Tex. Occ. Code §2155.052**, and a printed copy of the statute goes **on the door of each guest's sleeping room**. |

| A choking/Heimlich poster is required | **Not by state law.** Removed from the TFER pre-2015; zero hits for "chok" or "Heimlich" in current 25 TAC Ch. 228. Only a **first aid kit** is required (§228.211). Verify the City ordinance. |
| Texas requires CO alarms in hotel guest rooms | **It does not.** H&S Code Ch. 766 is public-awareness only. (The adopted IFC and Hilton standards may still require them.) |

## A. Human trafficking — mandatory, employee-facing

**Verified against the rule text.** 1 TAC §54.103, *"Human Trafficking Prevention Signs for
Commercial Lodging Establishments."*

| Spec | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Size | **At least 11" × 17"** |
| Font | **At least 16-point** |
| Languages | Posted **separately** in **English, Spanish, and any other primary language spoken by ≥10% of employees** |
| Location | **"In a location that is easily visible to all employees"** — back of house: break room, time clock, employee entrance. **Not a guest-facing sign.** |

Content must contain all four: (1) a statement that employees are required to receive annual
human-trafficking training and **may not be disciplined, retaliated against, or discriminated
against for a good-faith report**; (2) how to recognize and report trafficking, **including a
list of indicators**; (3) the reporting phone number; (4) DPS suspicious-activity contact.

⚠ **Trap:** the OAG's 8.5"×11" PDFs are **two pages meant to be taped together to make
11"×17"**. An 8.5"×11" sign alone **does not meet the statutory minimum**.

**Paired duty (no sign, but the sign asserts it):** every directly-employed employee must
complete **OAG-approved** training **annually**, **≥20 minutes**, new hires **within 90 days**.
Records retained **≥2 years** and produced to the OAG **within 72 hours** of request
(§§114.0051–114.0052, 1 TAC §54.101).

**Enforcement:** peace officers may enter 9–5 M–F to check. AG gives written notice + **30-day
cure**, then **up to $500 per violation, each day a separate violation** (§§114.0101–114.0103).

## B. Innkeeper notices — in the guest rooms

| # | Notice | Where | Requirement | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily room-rate card or sign** | **A conspicuous place in EACH of the 115 guest rooms** | Must state **the daily room rate** and **the date the card was posted**. A rate increase is **not effective until the 30th day** after a card showing it is posted — so post the **maximum/sell-out** rate. No prescribed wording. Misdemeanor, $25–$100 and/or up to 30 days. | Tex. Occ. Code **§2155.001(a),(b)**; penalty §2155.002 |
| 2 | **Room-rate ticket to the guest** | Handed over at check-in | Hotels with **20+ rooms** must give the guest a ticket showing the daily rate charged, conforming to the posted rate. Fine up to $100. | **§2155.001(c)**; penalty §2155.002(b),(e) |
| 3 | **Limited-liability-for-valuables notice** | **On the door of each guest's sleeping room** — the statute says "on the door" | A **printed copy of §2155.052 itself**. Posting is what caps liability at **$50**. Conditions: the hotel continuously maintains a metal safe/vault in good order, and keeps suitable locks/bolts on guest room doors and proper fastenings on transoms and windows. **Cap is lost** if a guest offered valuables for the safe and the hotel failed to deposit them and issue a receipt. | **§2155.052(b)(3)** |

Item 3 is technically conditional — not posting simply forfeits the cap — but **on 115 keys
that is a real exposure, so treat it as mandatory.** THLA's model heading is
**"LIMITED LIABILITY LAW."**

## C. Firearms — optional signs, mandatory constraint

⚠ **Read this before posting anything.** **Tex. Occ. Code §2155.1025** (SB 20, 87th Leg.,
eff. 9/1/2021): a hotel **may not** adopt a policy prohibiting a guest from —

- carrying or storing a firearm or ammunition **in the guest's room**;
- carrying it **directly en route to or from** the hotel or the room;
- carrying it **directly en route to or from the guest's vehicle** on hotel property,
  including guest parking;
- carrying or storing it **in the guest's vehicle** on hotel property.

A hotel **may** require that in **common areas** a handgun be carried **concealed**, or that a
firearm be carried **in a case or bag**.

**Consequence:** a §30.06/§30.07 sign at a hotel **cannot lawfully bar a guest** from any of
the above. Such signs are effective only as to non-guests, and only in common areas within
§2155.1025(b)'s limits. **Posting them without a written policy that carves out guests creates
legal exposure** — and triggers the **§2155.103** duty to publish the firearms policy on the
reservation website and in written booking confirmations (misdemeanor, fine up to $100).

If the owner still elects to post, the wording is fixed by statute:

**§30.06 (concealed):** *"Pursuant to Section 30.06, Penal Code (trespass by license holder
with a concealed handgun), a person licensed under Subchapter H, Chapter 411, Government Code
(handgun licensing law), may not enter this property with a concealed handgun"*

**§30.07 (open):** *"Pursuant to Section 30.07, Penal Code (trespass by license holder with an
openly carried handgun), a person licensed under Subchapter H, Chapter 411, Government Code
(handgun licensing law), may not enter this property with a handgun that is carried openly"*

**Format, both:** English **and** Spanish · **contrasting colors** · **block letters at least
one inch high** · conspicuous and clearly visible to the public · §30.07 additionally **at
each entrance**. ⚠ The statute requires Spanish but **does not prescribe the Spanish wording**
and there is no official state translation — have counsel approve the translation before
fabrication. §30.05 signs (which reach **non**-license holders under permitless carry) have no
prescribed wording.

## G. Elevator and boiler certificates — TDLR

**Elevator Certificate of Compliance** — must be posted in **one** of:

1. Inside the car, **7'0" max / 3'0" min** above the finished car floor; or
2. Outside the car in the **main lobby within 10 feet of the call button**, same heights; or
3. In a common-area lobby/hallway publicly accessible without assistance during all operating
   hours — **and identified by a directional plaque** mounted in the car or within 10 ft of the
   main-lobby call button, **font ≥18 points**, stating the equipment is **"Regulated by the
   Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation"** with **1-800-803-9202** and **building
   management's telephone number**.

Inspection every **12 months**; Category 5 full-load test every 5 years on traction elevators.
*(H&S Code §754.019(a)(4); 16 TAC §74.67.)* **H2SEP has 2 cabs** — see `PROJECT-FINDINGS.md`.

**Boiler / water-heater Certificate of Operation** — posted **"in a conspicuous place on or
near the boiler"** (Mech./Plumb. Room 030 or Mech. 025). Coverage triggers if heat input
**exceeds 200,000 BTU/hr**, water temperature exceeds **210 °F**, **or** nominal capacity
**exceeds 120 gallons**. ⚠ **The residential exemption in §755.022(b) does NOT apply to a
hotel.** Failure to post is a **Class A violation, $500–$3,000 first offense.**
*(H&S Code §755.029(c), §755.001(9),(12),(18); 16 TAC §65.61.)*

**Action:** pull the DHW submittals — input BTU/hr, storage gallons, setpoint. A commercial
plant for 115 keys commonly exceeds these thresholds. **Assume required until the submittals
prove otherwise.**

## I. Checked and found NOT to require signage

- **Smoke detectors** — Tex. H&S Code Ch. 792 imposes maintenance duties only: a working
  detector in every sleeping room, and **one hearing-impaired detector per 60 rooms or
  fraction, max 5**. At 115 keys that is **2**. ⚠ **Do not confuse this with ADA's 12
  communication-features rooms** — different law, different count; **ADA governs as the more
  stringent.** No signage requirement either way.
- **CO alarms** — not required by Texas law in hotel guest rooms.
- **Hotel occupancy tax** — no state posting requirement found in Tax Code Ch. 156.
- **Linen/towel reuse** — no statewide requirement. Some Texas cities require it via a
  TCEQ-filed drought contingency plan (30 TAC Ch. 288). **Verify Eagle Pass.**
- **HB 2015 / SB 1831 (88th Leg.)** — checked the enrolled text of both; neither creates any
  hotel signage requirement.
