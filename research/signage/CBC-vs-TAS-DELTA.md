# G401 signage delta — California CBC 11B (as drawn) vs 2012 TAS (as governing)

_H2SEP · Home2 Suites by Hilton · 115 keys · Eagle Pass, Maverick County, Texas._
_Researched 2026-08-20. Addresses `conflicts` **A10** (OPEN): G400/G401 cite California
CBC 11B on a Texas project; G001's Code Summary cites no accessibility standard at all._

> **Verification status.** The load-bearing claim below — that Texas has never required
> geometric restroom door symbols — was checked two independent ways: by full-text search
> of TDLR's official 2012 and 1994 TAS PDFs (with a control term to prove the extraction
> was valid), and by a separate direct read of TDLR's published TAS Chapter 7, which
> confirms 703.7.2 ends at `.4 Assistive Listening Systems`. Both agree.

## Bottom line

The single largest item on G401 — the 12-inch equilateral triangle / circle geometric
restroom door symbols — is a **California requirement that has never existed in Texas
law**. Almost nothing else on a correctly drawn CBC 11B sheet will technically *fail* a
TAS inspection, because CBC 11B meets or exceeds TAS on every character, Braille and
mounting metric. The real exposure is **parking signage**, where G401 posts a "Minimum
Fine $250" that is legally wrong in Texas (**$500–$750** first offense) and a tow sign
with California wording and dimensions that does not satisfy the Texas statute. The two
things that actually bite: the **Texas Occupations Code §2308.301 tow sign at every
vehicle entrance**, which appears on no California sheet and which the hotel needs in
order to legally tow anyone; and the process fact that a **Registered Accessibility
Specialist inspects against TAS only** — CBC 11B has no legal standing in Texas.

## Two corrections to what we assumed going in

**1. Texas has never required geometric restroom symbols — not even under the old TAS.**
It is widely repeated that the 1994 TAS carried one. It did not. TDLR's official 1994 TAS
(§4.30 Signage) runs 4.30.1 General · 4.30.2 Character Proportion · 4.30.3 Overhead ·
4.30.4 Raised/Braille · 4.30.5 Finish and Contrast · 4.30.6 Mounting · 4.30.7 Symbols of
Accessibility · 4.30.8 (reserved). **No geometric symbol provision, in either edition.**
Secondary sources get this wrong routinely — California's version is visually distinctive
enough that people assume it is national. Do not repeat the claim.

**2. The Texas accessible-parking statutes do not prescribe sign wording.** Tex. Transp.
Code **§681.009(b)** *delegates* to the TCLR standards (i.e. TAS) rather than dictating
text, and **§681.011** sets penalties without prescribing any sign copy. The real
Texas-only sign G401 will be missing lives in a different code entirely — **Tex. Occ. Code
§2308.301**, the tow-away sign.

## Item 1 in full — both code texts verbatim

**California, CBC 11B-703.7.2.6.1:** *"An equilateral triangle, ¼ inch (6.4 mm) thick with
edges 12 inches (305 mm) long and a vertex pointing upward, shall be located at entrances
to men's toilet and bathing facilities. The triangle symbol shall contrast with the door,
either light on a dark background or dark on a light background."* — with `.6.2` giving the
12" circle for women's and `.6.3` the inscribed combination for unisex, all scoped by
**11B-216.8.1**: *"Geometric symbols complying with Section 11B-703.7.2.6 shall be
provided at entrances to toilet and bathing rooms."*

**Texas, 2012 TAS 216.2:** *"Interior and exterior signs identifying permanent rooms and
spaces shall comply with 703.1, 703.2, and 703.5. Where pictograms are provided as
designations of permanent interior rooms and spaces, the pictograms shall comply with
703.6 and shall have text descriptors complying with 703.2 and 703.5."*

**TAS 703.7.2 Symbols** runs `.1` International Symbol of Accessibility · `.2` International
Symbol of TTY · `.3` Volume Control Telephones · `.4` Assistive Listening Systems.
**The list ends there.**

### What that means on the ground

The symbols are **not required** in Texas — but they are also **not prohibited**, and an
RAS will not write them up for being present. Two real consequences:

1. **Cost.** Custom ¼"-thick 12" geometric symbols at every public restroom door
   (019 WOMENS · 020 MEN'S · 027 Unisex, plus pool and fitness if shown) are a fabricated
   non-catalog item bought for zero Texas benefit.
2. **The dangerous failure mode.** In California the door symbol and the latch-side
   tactile sign are **two separate requirements**. If G401's schedule assumed the symbol
   carries the room identification, and the tactile sign is undersized, omitted, or
   mounted on the door instead of the latch wall, **that is a genuine TAS 216.2 / 703.4.2
   violation.** Check the schedule for a discrete latch-side tactile sign at every
   restroom, independent of any symbol.

## Add to G401 (Texas-required, currently missing)

### Tex. Occ. Code §2308.301 — tow-away sign at every vehicle entrance

The big omission, and invisible to a California-trained team because it lives in the
**Occupations Code**, not in any building or accessibility code.

- Minimum **18" wide × 24" tall**, weather-resistant, permanently mounted on pole, post,
  permanent wall or permanent barrier
- **Bottom edge no lower than 5 ft and no higher than 8 ft above ground**
- Contains the **international towing symbol**
- States **who may park** there and prohibits all others
- Bears the words **"Unauthorized Vehicles Will Be Towed or Booted at Owner's or
  Operator's Expense"** (or the tow-only / boot-only variants)
- Gives a **telephone number, with area code, answered 24 hours a day**
- **Placement:** facing and conspicuously visible to a driver entering, on the right or
  left of **each** driveway or curb cut; or where no definite entrances exist, at
  intervals such that **no entrance is more than 25 ft from a sign**

⚠ The RAS will **not** check this — it is outside TAS scope. It surfaces at operations,
or the first time the hotel tries to tow a car and finds it cannot.

### Correct Texas fine figures, if a deterrent sign is posted

No Texas statute *requires* posting the fine, but if the owner wants one, **Tex. Transp.
Code §681.011** is: no priors **$500–$750** · one prior $550–$800 + 10 hrs · two priors
$550–$800 + 20 hrs · three priors $800–$1,100 + 30 hrs · four or more **$1,250 + 50 hrs**.

### The enforcement trap — §681.011(f-2)

> "A peace officer may issue a warning but **may not issue a citation** for an offense"
> where the designated space lacks a parking space identification sign complying with the
> standards referred to in §681.009(b).

Because §681.009(b) points at TAS, **a parking sign that misses TAS 502.6 makes the space
legally unenforceable** — police can only warn, not ticket. Getting TAS 502.6 right is an
operational necessity, not a code checkbox.

## Safe to build as drawn

No redesign needed. Where CBC is stricter, a CBC-compliant sign is automatically
TAS-compliant, so the fabrication package can be reused as-is.

**Identical:** room ID scoping (216.2) · raised character depth, case, style, proportion,
height, stroke, spacing (703.2.1–.8) · Grade 2 Braille dot geometry and Table 703.3.1
(703.3.1) · tactile sign location incl. 18"×18" clear space (703.4.2) · visual character
finish, contrast, non-glare, Table 703.5.5 (703.5) · pictograms (703.6) · ISA, TTY,
volume-control and assistive-listening symbols (703.7.2.1–.4) · tactile exit scoping
(216.4.1) · accessible entrance ISA (216.6) · elevator jamb designations (407.2.3.1) ·
assistive listening signage (216.10) · "van accessible" + 60" min parking sign (502.6).

**CBC stricter, therefore safe:** Braille flush-left/centered with ½" max separation ·
48" measured to lowest Braille cell · blue/white ISA · reflectorized 70 sq in sign.

## Process — what happens at TDLR / RAS review

The project is governed by the **Texas Architectural Barriers Act, Tex. Gov't Code Ch.
469**, administered by TDLR. A 115-key hotel is far above the **$50,000** threshold, so it
**must** be registered with TDLR, plans **must** go to a Registered Accessibility
Specialist for review per **16 TAC §68.50**, and the completed building **must** be
inspected by a RAS, no later than **one year after the recorded completion date**. The
**owner** — not the architect, not the GC — is legally responsible under §469.101/§469.105.

**A RAS reviews and inspects against the 2012 TAS. Full stop.** CBC 11B has no legal
standing in Texas; an RAS will not open it. Compliance with CBC is neither a defense nor a
substitute. A sheet citing "CBC 11B" as the governing standard signals that the design
team applied the wrong code to the whole project — which invites a line-by-line review
instead of the routine pass a clean submittal gets, and is exactly the finding that gets
pulled in a later Title III claim.

**Would be cited:** missing/non-compliant tactile room ID at restrooms (216.2, 703.1/.2/.5)
· sign on the door leaf instead of the latch wall, or without 18"×18" clear space
(703.4.2) — *the most common real-world write-up* · mounting height outside 48"–60"
measured to the Texas datum (703.4.1) · parking sign bottom below 60" (502.6) · missing
"van accessible" (502.6) · missing tactile signs at exit passageway/discharge/stairway
doors (216.4.1) · Braille not contracted Grade 2 or dots not domed (703.3) · missing ISA
or directional signage (216.6, 216.3).

**Would NOT be cited:** absence of geometric symbols (not a TAS requirement) · presence of
them (permitted) · "Minimum Fine $250" signs (outside TAS scope — a liability problem, not
a TAS problem) · missing §2308.301 tow signs (outside TAS scope entirely; will not block
the TDLR file, but leaves the hotel unable to tow).

## Recommended sheet corrections before the TDLR submittal

1. **G001** — add the governing standard: *"2012 Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS),
   Texas Government Code Chapter 469, administered by TDLR."* Add the IBC edition the City
   of Eagle Pass has adopted.
2. **G400 / G401** — strike every "CBC 11B" reference; renumber all cited sections to their
   TAS equivalents. Retitle G401 *"Accessibility — Signage Standards (2012 TAS)."*
3. **G401** — delete the eleven California-only items; add the §2308.301 entrance tow sign
   detail with the 24-hour phone number; correct the fine figure if a deterrent sign stays.
4. **Sign schedule** — confirm a discrete latch-side tactile + Grade 2 Braille sign exists
   at **every** public restroom and **every** guest room, independent of any door symbol.
5. Engage the RAS **before** the drawings go to permit. A pre-submittal RAS review is far
   cheaper than field rework across 115 keys.
