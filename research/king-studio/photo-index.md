## How the room is oriented in these photographs

Entering from the corridor at the short end, the herringbone ACCENT WALL is on the LEFT (sofa, then
divider curtain, then bed headboard) and the KITCHENETTE and WORKING WALL run is on the RIGHT
(kitchen counter, desk with the two navy shelves, dresser with the TV). The window and the PTAC are
on the short wall at the far end, with the wardrobe / luggage unit beside them on the working wall
side. The bed head is against the accent wall and the bed projects across the room toward the
dresser and the TV.

## Notes and caveats

- All seven render views have a real match. Nothing was forced.
- **working is the weakest pairing.** No photograph is a square-on elevation of the working wall.
  photo-05 wins on content but rakes steeply away from the camera. If the render camera faces the
  wall instead, photo-03 gives the clearest read of the desk and BOTH navy floating shelves, and
  photo-12 or photo-13 give the same run seen from the bed end.
- **entry** assumes the render looks from the entry into the room. If it instead looks AT the
  corridor door from inside, swap to photo-10.
- **kitchen** alternates, if a wider view is wanted: photo-03 and photo-10.
- **bed** alternate, if the render looks from the lounge toward the window: photo-06.
- There is **no photograph dedicated to the window or the PTAC alone.** The window reads best in
  photo-07; the wardrobe reads best in photo-08.
- Site conditions in frame, not FF&E: a red-handled mop stands in the entry in photos 02, 10, 13 and
  14, and a worker in a hi-vis vest is reflected in the TV in photo-08 and clips the frame edge in
  photo-11.
- The mattresses have **no linens** in every photograph, so any render with made beds will not match.
- Room number 110 in pink marker is legible in photo-20 and photo-21.
